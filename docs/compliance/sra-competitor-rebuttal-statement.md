## Allegation A: "Unauthorised Practice of Reserved Legal Activities (Legal Services Act 2007 ss. 12–14)"

### The Competitors' Assertion
*Certain complainant firms allege that SettlementCheck's interactive calculators, "verdicts", and assessment tools evaluate legal claims and generate settlement documentation, thereby conducting reserved legal activities without authorization under Sections 12, 13, and 14 of the Legal Services Act 2007 (LSA 2007).*

### Legal & Factual Rebuttal

#### 1. Exhaustive Statutory Scope of Reserved Legal Activities (LSA 2007 s.12)
Section 12(1) of the Legal Services Act 2007 defines reserved legal activities exhaustively as:
- *(a) the exercise of a right of audience;*
- *(b) the conduct of litigation;*
- *(c) reserved instrument activities;*
- *(d) probate activities;*
- *(e) notarial activities; and*
- *(f) the administration of oaths.*

SettlementCheck performs **none** of these activities:
- It does not represent parties before any court or tribunal (no right of audience);
- It does not issue proceedings, file claims, or correspond with tribunal offices (no conduct of litigation);
- It does not draft deeds or instruments under the Land Registration Act 2002 or Law of Property Act 1925 (no reserved instrument activities);
- It does not handle probate, oaths, or notarial instruments.

Under long-standing common law authority, general legal information, decision-support tools, and mathematical estimations do not constitute reserved legal activities.

#### 2. Pure Algorithmic Execution of Published Statutory Instruments
SettlementCheck executes pure deterministic arithmetic derived directly from parliamentary statutes and statutory instruments:
- **Statutory Redundancy Pay**: Calculated strictly in accordance with Section 162 and Section 227 of the Employment Rights Act 1996 (ERA 1996) and the Employment Rights (Increase of Limits) Order 2026 (SI 2026/310: GB weekly cap £751; NI cap £783);
- **Statutory Notice Pay**: Calculated strictly under Section 86 ERA 1996;
- **Unfair Dismissal Statutory Ceiling**: Enforced at the lower of 52 weeks' actual pay or the statutory ceiling of £123,543 pursuant to ERA 1996 s.124(1ZA) and SI 2026/310;
- **Post-Employment Notice Pay (PENP)**: Computed verbatim via the statutory algebraic formula set out in Section 402D of the Income Tax (Earnings and Pensions) Act 2003 (ITEPA 2003): `((BP × D) / P) - T`.

The mathematical application of an enacted parliamentary formula to user-entered figures (salary, age, years of service) does not constitute legal advice. If performing statutory arithmetic constituted an unauthorized legal activity, every online payroll calculator, tax table, and government benefit estimator would be unlawful.

#### 3. Complete Elimination of Prescriptive Direction
Unlike uncalibrated systems, SettlementCheck enforces strict descriptive framing. As evidenced by [`lib/agentic-engine/guardrails/sra-boundary-guard.ts`](file:///c:/settlementcheck/lib/agentic-engine/guardrails/sra-boundary-guard.ts):
- The platform never instructs users: *"you should ask for"*, *"do not sign"*, or *"we advise you to"*;
- All outputs are framed as *"Calculated statutory baselines (subject to eligibility)"* and *"Illustrative negotiation figures"*;
- Every results page provides a clear boundary disclaimer emphasizing that under Section 203(3) ERA 1996, legally binding advice can only be provided by an instructed independent SRA-regulated solicitor.

## Allegation B: "Unlawful Referral Fee or Improper Steering Under SRA Code of Conduct Rule 5.1"

### The Competitors' Assertion
*Complainants allege that SettlementCheck operates an unlawful referral scheme that steers vulnerable consumers to preferred solicitors in exchange for improper financial kickbacks, compromising solicitor independence.*

### Legal & Factual Rebuttal

#### 1. Compliance with SRA Code of Conduct Paragraph 5.1
Paragraph 5.1 of the SRA Code of Conduct for Solicitors, RELs and RFLs permits introductions provided:
1. Clients are informed in writing of the financial arrangement;
2. The solicitor's independence and professional judgment remain uncompromised; and
3. The arrangement does not breach the Legal Aid, Sentencing and Punishment of Offenders Act 2012 (LASPO) referral fee ban (which applies strictly to personal injury claims, not employment matters).

#### 2. Transparent Fixed Administrative Technology Fee Model
SettlementCheck operates under an administrative fee structure that complies with SRA Standards:
- **Zero Cost to Consumer**: The employee is charged £0 by SettlementCheck.
- **Fixed Administrative Technology Fee**: The participating SRA-regulated firm pays a fixed administrative onboarding and data intake fee of £95 + VAT per introduction.
- **No Commission / No Outcome Contingency**: The fee is 100% fixed. It is payable whether or not the client enters into a retainer, whether or not a settlement agreement is executed, and regardless of the settlement sum obtained. SettlementCheck derives zero financial benefit from higher settlement amounts, preserving complete neutrality.
- **Pre-Capture Disclosure**: Users are informed of this exact financial mechanism prior to submitting their contact details, satisfying SRA Rule 5.1 in full.

#### 3. Uncompromised Solicitor Independence & Absolute Freedom of Choice
Under the legally binding Panel Addendum:
- Participating solicitors retain exclusive, unfettered responsibility for advising their clients. SettlementCheck has zero access to confidential legal consultations or case files;
- Users are repeatedly reminded across the site that they are under no obligation to use an introduced panel solicitor and may consult any independent solicitor, Law Centre, or trade union representative.

## Allegation C: "Misleading Marketing Regarding Free Advice (DMCC Act 2024 / CAP Code Rule 3.38)"

### The Competitors' Assertion
*Complainants allege that SettlementCheck misleads consumers by claiming legal advice is "100% free" and that employers are legally obligated to pay all solicitor fees under Section 203 ERA 1996, in violation of the Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act 2024 (DMCC Act 2024) and CAP Code Rule 3.38.*

### Legal & Factual Rebuttal

#### 1. Proactive Remediation & Total Decommissioning of "Free Advice" Claims
Digitally Swift Ltd acknowledges that in the digital legal sector, the phrase *"your employer pays your legal fee"* is frequently used without necessary nuance. Recognizing this industry-wide ambiguity, SettlementCheck undertook a total codebase audit, purging all unqualified claims of "free" advice.

#### 2. Precise Explanation of Statutory Conditions vs Commercial Conventions
The platform now provides an educational explanation of legal fee mechanics across all user touchpoints:
- **The Statutory Reality**: Section 203(3) ERA 1996 establishes independent legal advice as a mandatory validity requirement for waiving statutory rights, but the statute does not impose an obligation on the employer to fund that advice.
- **The Commercial Convention**: In standard UK practice, employers customarily agree to pay a capped financial contribution (typically £350 to £500+ plus VAT) directly to the employee's solicitor upon successful execution of the agreement.
- **Transparency on Residual Costs**: The platform explicitly informs users that if negotiation extends beyond the employer's contribution cap, or if negotiations abort without a signed agreement, the employee may be liable for additional fees in accordance with the solicitor's engagement letter.

This standard sets a benchmark for CMA DMCC Act 2024 and CAP Code 3.38 transparency within the legal technology sector.

## Allegation D: "Endangering Employee Positions Through Uncalibrated Negotiation Guidance"

### The Competitors' Assertion
*Complainants allege that SettlementCheck encourages employees to reject settlement offers and make aggressive demands to HR, risking immediate revocation of existing offers under common law principles (*Hyde v Wrench*) and misunderstanding the non-binding nature of ACAS Code 4 deadlines.*

### Legal & Factual Rebuttal

#### 1. Eradication of Direct-to-HR Communication Templates
SettlementCheck has completely eradicated all functionality or copy that encouraged unrepresented employees to email negotiation demands or "Without Prejudice proposals" directly to HR:
- All generated documentation has been re-architected into a **"Solicitor Discussion Agenda & Case Brief"**;
- Prominent notices instruct users:
  > *"DO NOT send this document to your employer or HR. This agenda is prepared exclusively for your initial consultation with your instructed SRA-regulated solicitor, who will evaluate legal risk, preserve legal privilege, and guide strategic communications."*

#### 2. Prominent Legal Invariant Warnings

##### A. Offer Revocation Risk (*Hyde v Wrench* [1840] 3 Beav 334)
The platform alerts users that under basic English contract law (*Hyde v Wrench*), putting forward a counter-proposal terminates the original offer. If an employer's offer is revoked following an unadvised counter-offer, the employee cannot subsequently accept the original offer as of right.

##### B. Offer Withdrawal (*Routledge v Grant* [1828] 4 Bing 653)
Users are explicitly warned that an employer is entitled to withdraw a settlement offer at any time prior to formal execution, notwithstanding any informal deadline previously discussed.

##### C. ACAS Code of Practice 4 Paragraph 12 Calibration
The platform accurately describes the 10-calendar-day ACAS consideration period not as an immutable statutory right, but as an evidentiary benchmark. Under ERA 1996 s.111A(4), giving an employee insufficient time to consider an offer without justification can constitute "improper behaviour", removing statutory confidentiality from pre-termination discussions in ordinary unfair dismissal proceedings.

##### D. Protection of Legal Privilege (*Rush & Tompkins* / *Barnetson*)
Users are alerted that Section 111A ERA 1996 does not protect discussions relating to discrimination, whistleblowing, or breach of contract, and that common law Without Prejudice privilege requires an existing dispute. The platform actively steers users away from unadvised disclosures that could compromise their legal privilege.
