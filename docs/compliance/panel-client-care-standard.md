# Standardized Client Care & Section 203 Fee Disclosure Standard

**Document Reference:** `docs/compliance/panel-client-care-standard.md`  
**Governing Authorities:** SRA Code of Conduct (Rules 5.1, 8.6, 8.7), Section 203(3) Employment Rights Act 1996, Consumer Rights Act 2015  
**Target Audience:** Panel Law Firms, Compliance Officers for Legal Practice (COLPs), Employment Fee Earners  
**Status:** Mandatory Minimum Operational Standard  

---

## 1. Executive Summary & Purpose

This Standard establishes the mandatory requirements for Client Care Letters (CCLs) and fee disclosures issued by SRA-regulated Panel Law Firms to employee clients introduced via SettlementCheck.co.uk.

The primary objectives of this Standard are:
1. **Eliminate Client Fee Surprises**: Ensure vulnerable employees under workplace stress are never blindsided by unexpected legal bills or unclear billing terms.
2. **Clarify the Employer Fee Contribution**: Eliminate the pervasive consumer misconception that the employer legal fee contribution is an automatic statutory right, establishing clearly that it is a commercial contractual convention.
3. **Enforce SRA Transparency Rules**: Provide seamless compliance with SRA Code of Conduct Rules 5.1 (introducers), 8.6 (costs information), and 8.7 (complaints procedures).
4. **Safeguard Section 203(3) Enforceability**: Ensure that independent legal advice satisfies all statutory prerequisites under the Employment Rights Act 1996.

### 2.1 Scope of Retainer (Statutory Advisory Perimeter)
The Client Care Letter must clearly define the precise scope of legal advice:
1. **Statutory Requirement**: Advising the employee on the terms and effect of the proposed agreement, with particular reference to its effect on the employee's ability to pursue their rights before an Employment Tribunal (ERA 1996 s.203(3)(b)).
2. **Substantive Merits Review**:
   - Evaluating the adequacy of the financial consideration (ex-gratia termination payment, notice pay, statutory redundancy entitlement).
   - Scrutinizing the scope of the waiver of claims in light of *Bathgate v Technip Singapore PTE Ltd [2023]* and *BCCI v Ali [2001]* to ensure unlawful blanket waivers of unknown claims are excised or qualified.
   - Reviewing contractual warranties, tax indemnities under ITEPA 2003 s.403 and Post-Employment Notice Pay (PENP) under s.402D, and post-termination restrictive covenants.
   - Verifying the inclusion of an agreed factual reference and non-disparagement provisions.

### 2.2 SRA Rule 5.1 Introducer Disclosure Clause
The CCL must contain an unambiguous written disclosure of the introduction pursuant to SRA Code of Conduct Rule 5.1(c):

> **Introducer Disclosure Clause:**  
> "You were introduced to our firm through SettlementCheck.co.uk, an independent website operated by Digitally Swift Ltd. We confirm that our firm pays SettlementCheck a fixed administrative technology fee of [insert agreed fee, e.g., £50.00 + VAT] for the secure verification and transmission of your intake dossier. This fee is a fixed administrative cost borne entirely by our firm; it is never charged to you, is not deducted from your settlement compensation, and is payable irrespective of whether your settlement agreement completes. Digitally Swift Ltd is not an authorized law firm and exercises zero control, direction, or influence over our professional advice. We owe our undivided duty of care solely to you, and you remain completely free to instruct any solicitor of your choosing."

## 3. The Employer Fee Contribution: Commercial Reality & Limits

### 3.1 Demystifying the "Free Legal Advice" Myth
Consumers frequently believe that Section 203(3) ERA 1996 grants employees a statutory right to free legal advice funded by employers. This is legally incorrect and creates dangerous client friction if not clarified upfront.

### 3.2 Mandatory CCL Explanation of Employer Contribution
The Panel Firm must include the following explanation in the CCL:

> **Explanation of Employer Contribution Clause:**  
> "Under Section 203(3) of the Employment Rights Act 1996, a settlement agreement is only legally binding if you take advice from a qualified independent adviser. In UK employment practice, employers customarily agree to pay a commercial contribution towards your legal fees (typically between £350 and £500+ +VAT, or £750+ +VAT for senior exits) upon final execution of the agreement.  
> 
> However, please note that:  
> 1. **Commercial Term, Not Statutory Right:** The employer fee contribution is a negotiated commercial term of the draft contract; it is not an automatic entitlement under statute.  
> 2. **Payable Upon Completion:** Employers only pay this contribution if the agreement is fully signed and completed. If negotiations break down, or if you decide not to sign the agreement and pursue an Employment Tribunal claim instead, the employer is usually not obliged to pay this contribution unless they agree to do so in separate without-prejudice terms.  
> 3. **Primary Liability:** Under English law, as the client instructing us, you remain primarily responsible for our legal fees. However, our transparent fee structure below is designed so that you do not incur personal liability without your prior express agreement."

## 4. Upfront Transparent Fee Structure & Cap Notification

### 4.1 The "No Surprise Fees" Standard
To protect introduced employees, Panel Firms must offer a transparent, tiered fee structure that guarantees zero out-of-pocket expenses for standard review services.

### 4.2 Tier 1: Standard Review & Certification (Cap-Covered)
Where an employee is satisfied with their severance terms and requires the statutory review, advice on effects, and Section 203(3) certification:
- The Panel Firm must offer a **fixed fee equal to the employer's agreed legal fee contribution** (e.g. £500 + VAT).
- The firm undertakes to invoice the employer directly upon completion.
- The employee pays **£0.00 out of pocket**.

### 4.3 Tier 2: Enhanced Negotiation & Dispute Resolution (Cap Exceedance)
Where the employee requests substantive negotiations to increase the ex-gratia amount, contest disciplinary allegations, or amend restrictive covenants:
1. **Pre-Agreement on Increased Contribution**: The solicitor must first attempt to secure the employer's agreement to increase their legal fee contribution (e.g., from £500 to £1,000 + VAT) to cover additional negotiation time.
2. **Mandatory Advance Notification**: If work is expected to exceed the employer's contribution cap, the solicitor must:
   - Provide a written estimate or fixed fee quote for the additional work before undertaking it.
   - Specify the exact basis of charging (e.g., hourly rate of £[X] + VAT or fixed negotiation fee of £[Y] + VAT).
   - Obtain the client's explicit written approval before incurring any fees that would fall outside the employer's contribution.
3. **Strict Ban on Retrospective Invoicing**: Panel Firms are strictly prohibited from billing an introduced client for fees exceeding the employer contribution unless that client provided explicit prior written consent to the specific additional expenditure.

## 6. Regulatory Escalation, Complaints & Cancellation Rights

### 6.1 Complaints Procedure (SRA Rule 8.7)
Every CCL must clearly detail the firm's internal complaints handling procedure, including:
- Name and contact details of the designated Complaints Handling Partner / COLP.
- Right to escalate unresolved complaints to the **Legal Ombudsman (LeO)** within 6 months of the firm's final written response.
- Right to report regulatory breaches to the **Solicitors Regulation Authority (SRA)**.

### 6.2 Consumer Cancellation Rights (Distance Contracts)
Where instructions are taken online or over the telephone without face-to-face meetings:
1. The client has a statutory 14-day cooling-off period under the Consumer Contracts (Information, Cancellation and Additional Charges) Regulations 2013.
2. Because settlement agreements frequently operate under urgent statutory or Acas deadlines (e.g., the ACAS 10-calendar-day consideration guideline), the CCL must include an explicit **Express Request for Urgent Performance**:
   - The client explicitly requests that legal work commences immediately during the 14-day cancellation period.
   - The client acknowledges that if the service is fully completed within the 14-day period, their right to cancel will be lost.

1. SettlementCheck Compliance Team performs quarterly sample audits of redacted Client Care Letters issued to platform users to verify inclusion of mandatory SRA Rule 5.1 and fee transparency disclosures.
2. Any Panel Firm failing to include required disclosures or found charging unapproved excess fees will receive an immediate formal warning and may be suspended from the platform panel.
