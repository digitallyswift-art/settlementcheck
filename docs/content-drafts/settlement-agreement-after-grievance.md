# Settlement Agreement After Raising a Grievance: UK Employee Rights & Payouts (2026)

Raising a formal workplace grievance takes considerable courage. When your employer responds by proposing a settlement agreement or calling an off-the-record meeting, it can feel unsettling. This guide outlines your legal rights, statutory negotiation baselines, and how compensation is calculated under 2026 UK employment law.

Figures on this page reflect the Employment Rights (Increase of Limits) Order 2026 (SI 2026/310), in force from 6 April 2026. Last reviewed: November 2026.

## Statutory Quick Answer (ACAS Code 1 & ERA 1996 Rules)

When an employer proposes a settlement agreement after a grievance, they seek a binding commercial exit to avoid formal investigation, management disruption, and tribunal exposure. You are under no legal obligation to accept or withdraw your grievance. Typical settlements include contractual notice, accrued holiday, and tax-free ex-gratia compensation reflecting potential tribunal liabilities.

- **Statutory Framework:** ERA 1996 s.203 & s.111A (Binding exit waiver rules)
- **Retaliation Protection:** Equality Act 2010 s.27 (Victimisation protection)
- **ACAS Consideration Window:** 10 Calendar Days (Guidance under ACAS Code 4)
- **Employer Legal Contribution:** Typically £350 to £500+ plus VAT upon completion

## Core rules every employee must know

- Proposing a settlement agreement does not automatically pause or cancel your formal grievance unless mutually agreed.
- Section 111A confidentiality does not protect discussions involving discrimination, harassment, victimisation, or whistleblowing.
- Raising a grievance concerning discrimination is a protected act under Section 27 of the Equality Act 2010, shielding you against unlawful victimisation.
- An unreasonable failure by an employer to follow the ACAS Code of Practice 1 can increase tribunal awards by up to 25% under Section 207A TULRCA 1992.
- Genuine compensatory termination payments are tax-free up to £30,000 under Section 403 ITEPA 2003, whereas notice pay is fully taxable under Section 402D.
- Employers customarily provide a capped fee contribution (typically £350 to £500+ plus VAT) directly to your independent solicitor upon completion.

## Why employers propose settlement agreements after a grievance

When you lodge a formal grievance, you trigger strict procedural obligations under the ACAS Code of Practice 1 on Disciplinary and Grievance Procedures. Your employer must appoint an impartial investigator, gather witness statements, hold formal meetings, and produce a written outcome.

This internal process carries substantial operational friction. Managers must dedicate working hours to investigations, internal correspondence becomes subject to Data Protection Act subject access requests, and team relationships frequently deteriorate.

Moreover, grievances rarely restore harmonious working dynamics. If your employer upholds your grievance, they formally admit internal failings or manager misconduct. If they dismiss your grievance, you may feel compelled to resign and claim constructive dismissal under Section 95(1)(c) of the Employment Rights Act 1996. Proposing an agreed exit under Section 203 ERA 1996 allows your employer to achieve commercial finality.

## Do you have to accept the offer or drop your grievance?

You are under zero legal obligation to accept a settlement agreement. Nor are you required to withdraw your grievance simply because settlement discussions have begun.

A settlement agreement is a voluntary contract. Your employer cannot lawfully dismiss you on the spot simply for declining an offer. If negotiations do not reach an agreement, your employment continues and your employer remains bound to conclude your grievance investigation.

> **Important procedural safeguard:** Never withdraw or drop a formal grievance before a binding settlement agreement is fully executed and certified by your instructed solicitor. Withdrawing your grievance prematurely forfeits your formal standing under ACAS Code 1 and eliminates key legal protection against retaliation.

When settlement discussions begin, you and your solicitor can adopt one of two recognised procedural pathways:

1. **Agreed temporary pause:** Both parties agree in writing to pause the grievance timetable for a defined period (such as 10 to 14 days) to focus on settlement terms. If discussions stall, the grievance timetable resumes immediately.
2. **Twin-track process:** The formal grievance investigation proceeds on an open basis, while negotiations progress concurrently on a confidential without prejudice track. This maintains procedural pressure on your employer to resolve the dispute.

## The confidentiality trap: Section 111A vs Without Prejudice

Employers often invite employees to off-the-record meetings to discuss settlement terms. However, UK employment law governs confidential discussions under two very different legal frameworks.

The distinction between statutory protected conversations under Section 111A ERA 1996 and common law Without Prejudice privilege is critical when a grievance has been raised.

| Legal Dimension | Section 111A Protected Conversation | Common Law Without Prejudice |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Statutory Basis** | Employment Rights Act 1996 s.111A | Court precedent (Rush & Tompkins) |
| **Existing Dispute Needed?** | No prior dispute needed | Yes, requires an active legal dispute |
| **Claims Protected** | Ordinary unfair dismissal only | Wide civil & tribunal causes of action |
| **Discrimination Protection** | Excluded (Inadmissible) | Covered, if genuine settlement effort |
| **Loss of Protection** | Improper behaviour (s.111A(4)) | Unambiguous impropriety (fraud/abuse) |

### Why Section 111A fails to protect employers in grievance disputes

Section 111A of the Employment Rights Act 1996 was created to allow employers to hold confidential pre-termination negotiations. However, Section 111A protection applies strictly to ordinary unfair dismissal claims.

If your grievance raises allegations of unlawful discrimination under the Equality Act 2010, sexual harassment, or whistleblowing under ERA 1996 s.43B, Section 111A provides zero confidentiality. What your employer says in that meeting can be disclosed directly to an employment tribunal.

Furthermore, under Section 111A(4), confidentiality is waived if your employer engages in improper behaviour. Setting unworkably tight deadlines, threatening immediate dismissal if an offer is declined, or applying aggressive pressure can forfeit statutory protection entirely.

### How Without Prejudice applies after a grievance

Common law Without Prejudice privilege prevents statements made in a genuine attempt to settle an existing dispute from being put before a tribunal. Unlike Section 111A, Without Prejudice covers discrimination and breach of contract claims.

Submitting a formal grievance generally establishes that a genuine dispute exists between the parties. However, Without Prejudice cannot be used as a cloak for discriminatory threats or retaliation under the doctrine of unambiguous impropriety.

## Section 27 Equality Act 2010: Protection against victimisation

If your grievance concerns discrimination, harassment, or a failure to make reasonable adjustments for a disability, you receive immediate protection under Section 27 of the Equality Act 2010.

Submitting a grievance that alleges unlawful discrimination is a protected act under Section 27(2). It is unlawful for your employer to subject you to any detriment because you did a protected act.

Unlawful detriments include disciplinary threats, ostracisation, adverse performance ratings, or threats of dismissal following your grievance. Protection against victimisation applies from day one of employment, without requiring two years of continuous service.

Victimisation claims in an employment tribunal carry uncapped financial compensation. This statutory protection provides vital context when reviewing any settlement offer with an instructed solicitor.

## How grievance settlements are calculated under 2026 rules

A fair settlement calculation reflects your contractual entitlements, statutory baselines, and the commercial exposure your employer avoids by settling.

Under the Employment Rights (Increase of Limits) Order 2026 (SI 2026/310), in force from 6 April 2026, the statutory weekly pay cap is £751 in Great Britain (£783 in Northern Ireland). The maximum statutory unfair dismissal compensatory award is £123,543.

| Payment Component | Statutory Basis / Cap | Tax Treatment |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Contractual Notice Pay (PILON)** | Contract of employment / ERA 1996 s.86 | Taxable as earnings (s.402D PENP) |
| **Accrued Untaken Holiday** | Working Time Regulations 1998 reg.14 | Taxable as earnings |
| **Calculated Redundancy Baseline** | £751/week (max £22,530 under SI 2026/310) | Tax-free (s.403 ITEPA) |
| **Ex-Gratia Compensation** | Commercial negotiation / £123,543 cap | Tax-free up to £30,000 |
| **ACAS Code 1 Uplift** | Up to 25% under s.207A TULRCA 1992 | Included in compensatory sum |
| **Injury to Feelings (Vento)** | Ninth Addendum (£1,300 to £62,900+) | Tax-free if pre-termination (s.406) |

### The ACAS Code 1 uplift of up to 25% (s.207A TULRCA 1992)

Under Section 207A of the Trade Union and Labour Relations (Consolidation) Act 1992, an employment tribunal has statutory power to adjust compensation by up to 25% if an employer unreasonably fails to comply with the ACAS Code of Practice 1.

Common employer failures include unreasonable delays in scheduling grievance meetings, refusing to allow a trade union representative or colleague to accompany the employee, or failing to offer an independent appeal. Instructed solicitors factor this potential 25% uplift into negotiation models.

### Injury to feelings and the Ninth Addendum Vento bands

If your grievance involves unlawful discrimination, harassment, or victimisation, compensation is not capped by the unfair dismissal limits. Tribunals award injury to feelings compensation assessed under the Presidential Guidance Ninth Addendum:

- **Lower Band (£1,300 to £12,600):** Applies to isolated or less serious incidents of unlawful discrimination.
- **Middle Band (£12,600 to £37,700):** Applies to serious cases that do not merit an award in the highest bracket, such as persistent bullying or repeated discriminatory remarks.
- **Upper Band (£37,700 to £62,900):** Reserved for the most serious cases, including lengthy campaigns of discriminatory harassment. Exceptional cases can exceed £62,900.

## Tax rules: The £30,000 exemption vs Post-Employment Notice Pay

Understanding how your settlement payment is taxed ensures you evaluate the true net take-home figure.

Under Section 403 of the Income Tax (Earnings and Pensions) Act 2003 (ITEPA 2003), genuine ex-gratia compensation for loss of office is exempt from income tax and National Insurance contributions up to £30,000.

However, notice pay cannot be disguised as tax-free compensation. Under Section 402D ITEPA 2003, Post-Employment Notice Pay (PENP) rules require that any unworked portion of your contractual notice period must be taxed in full as earnings.

Where a grievance involves discrimination, compensation paid for injury to feelings that occurred prior to and unconnected with the termination of employment may be paid free of tax under Section 406 ITEPA 2003, without consuming the £30,000 threshold.

## Independent legal advice and solicitor fee contributions

Under Section 203(3) of the Employment Rights Act 1996, an employee cannot contract out of their statutory rights unless six mandatory legal conditions are satisfied:

1. The agreement must be in writing.
2. The agreement must relate to particular proceedings or complaints.
3. The employee must have received advice from an independent, qualified adviser.
4. The adviser must hold a valid contract of insurance covering professional liability.
5. The adviser must be identified in the agreement.
6. The agreement must state that the statutory conditions regulating settlement agreements are met.

Because independent advice is a mandatory legal requirement for your employer to obtain a valid waiver, employers standardly provide a capped fee contribution directly to your solicitor upon completion.

In standard UK practice, this contribution typically ranges from £350 to £500+ plus VAT. For a standard review and sign-off within this contribution, the employer contribution covers the solicitor fee. If extensive negotiations are required, your solicitor can request an increased contribution from your employer. Any advice exceeding the agreed cap or abortive work remains subject to the solicitor terms of engagement.

## Frequently asked questions

### Why did my employer propose a settlement agreement after I submitted a formal grievance?
Employers often propose settlement agreements to avoid lengthy, disruptive workplace investigations. Under the ACAS Code of Practice 1, handling a grievance requires significant management time, witness interviews, and formal hearings. An agreed exit under Section 203 of the Employment Rights Act 1996 provides both parties with a clean, legally binding conclusion.

### Do I have to accept the settlement agreement or withdraw my grievance?
No. You are under no legal obligation to accept a settlement agreement or withdraw your grievance. Settlement agreements are voluntary contracts. If you decline the offer, your employment continues and your employer remains legally obligated to investigate your grievance properly under ACAS standards.

### What happens to my grievance investigation while settlement terms are discussed?
Parties typically agree to either pause the grievance investigation during negotiations or operate a twin-track approach. In a twin-track process, the formal grievance continues on an open basis while discussions proceed confidentially on a without prejudice basis. Never withdraw a grievance before an agreement is fully signed and certified by your solicitor.

### Is a settlement conversation confidential if my grievance involves discrimination or whistleblowing?
No. Section 111A of the Employment Rights Act 1996 only protects pre-termination negotiations from being disclosed in ordinary unfair dismissal claims. Under Section 111A(4), statutory confidentiality does not apply to discrimination claims under the Equality Act 2010 or whistleblowing disclosures under ERA 1996 s.43B.

### How is a grievance settlement payout calculated under 2026 rules?
A settlement package typically comprises contractual notice pay, accrued untaken holiday, and tax-free ex-gratia compensation up to £30,000 under Section 403 ITEPA 2003. Where discrimination is alleged, compensation may include injury to feelings under the Ninth Addendum Vento bands (£1,300 to £62,900+). Unreasonable failure by an employer to follow the ACAS Code can also trigger an uplift of up to 25% under Section 207A TULRCA 1992.

### Does my employer contribute to my independent legal fees?
Yes. Under Section 203(3) of the Employment Rights Act 1996, you must receive independent legal advice from an insured solicitor for the agreement to be valid. In standard UK practice, employers customarily provide a capped fee contribution (typically £350 to £500+ plus VAT) directly to your solicitor upon completion.
