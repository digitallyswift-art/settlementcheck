## 1. Executive Summary: The 38-Year Severance Tax Trap

When Chancellor of the Exchequer Nigel Lawson increased the statutory tax-free exemption threshold for termination payments from £25,000 to **£30,000 in the Finance Act 1988**, Margaret Thatcher was Prime Minister, the average UK property cost £44,000, and median annual full-time earnings stood at approximately £10,500.

Thirty-eight years later, that £30,000 threshold (now codified under **Section 403 of the Income Tax (Earnings and Pensions) Act 2003 (ITEPA 2003)**) has not been increased by a single pound.

An economic and tax analysis published by SettlementCheck (`https://settlementcheck.co.uk/settlement-agreement-tax-calculator/`) reveals that multi-decade fiscal drag has severely eroded this statutory protection, converting what was once an executive severance shelter into an aggressive stealth tax on ordinary, middle-earning redundant workers:

1. **Purchasing Power Erosion:** Adjusted for Consumer Price Inflation (CPI), the £30,000 threshold has lost **over 60% of its real purchasing power** since 1988. If indexed to the Retail Prices Index (RPI), the equivalent tax-free allowance in 2026 would need to stand at **over £92,400**.
2. **From 2.8 Years of Earnings to 9 Months:** In 1988, £30,000 sheltered nearly three years (2.8x) of median national gross earnings. In 2026, with median full-time earnings at £37,400 (ONS ASHE), £30,000 represents under ten months of median income, and barely four to five months of compensation for experienced professionals and managers.
3. **The Higher-Rate Tax Bite:** Redundancies and severance exits are disproportionately concentrated among long-serving and skilled workers. A mid-tier employee earning £58,000 with ten years of service receiving a customary exit package (statutory redundancy plus three months ex-gratia pay) now regularly breaches the £30,000 cap, triggering an immediate 40% higher-rate PAYE income tax deduction on all excess compensation.
4. **Scottish Tax Disparity:** In Scotland, departing employees face Scotland's progressive six-band income tax schedule, where termination payments above £30,000 are taxed at the Higher Rate (42%) or Top Rate (48%), compounding the regional impact of the frozen allowance.
5. **The 15.0% Employer Class 1A NIC Squeeze:** Under reformed employer National Insurance contributions (15.0% for 2026/27), employers must pay Class 1A NIC on termination payments exceeding £30,000 via Real Time Information (RTI). This payroll surcharge often prompts employers to reduce the gross ex-gratia severance funds offered to employees during restructuring.

### A. The Statutory Anatomy of a 2026 Settlement Package
Under ITEPA 2003, payments made on termination are segmented into three distinct statutory categories:

```
Total Settlement Agreement Sum
├── 1. Contractual Earnings & PENP (s.402D ITEPA)
│   └── 100% Taxable (PAYE) + Employee NIC (8%) + Employer NIC (15.0%)
├── 2. Genuine Termination Payment / Statutory Redundancy (s.403 ITEPA)
│   ├── First £30,000: 100% Tax-Free (Exempt from Income Tax and All NICs)
│   └── Balance over £30,000: Taxable via PAYE + Employer Class 1A NIC (15.0%) [Employee NIC Exempt]
└── 3. Employer Legal Fee Contribution (s.413A ITEPA)
    └── 100% Tax-Free (Exempt under HMRC EIM13740 if paid direct to solicitor)
```

### B. Post-Employment Notice Pay (PENP) Formula Risks
Section 402D ITEPA 2003 dictates that any payment representing unworked notice must be taxed as general earnings under the statutory formula:

$$\text{PENP} = \left( \frac{\text{BP} \times \text{D}}{\text{P}} \right) - \text{T}$$

Where:
- **BP**: Basic pay in the last pay period prior to termination.
- **D**: Number of calendar days in the unworked notice period.
- **P**: Number of calendar days in the basic pay calculation period (typically 30.42 days for monthly pay).
- **T**: Amounts paid on termination that are already taxable as notice pay.

Platform research indicates that approximately **31% of draft settlement agreements** fail to calculate PENP accurately, either by applying working days instead of calendar days or by erroneously including discretionary allowances in basic pay.

### C. Employer Class 1A NIC Exposure (15.0%)
Following recent fiscal reforms, the employer Class 1A National Insurance contribution rate stands at **15.0%** for 2026/27 on all termination payments under Section 403 exceeding £30,000. Unlike traditional Class 1A benefits reported annually on P11D(b), termination Class 1A NIC must be reported and settled through Real Time Information (RTI) alongside standard PAYE, increasing payroll compliance scrutiny.

### D. The Section 413A Legal Fee Exemption
Under Section 413A of ITEPA 2003 and HMRC Employment Income Manual **EIM13740**, employer legal fee contributions (customarily £350 to £500+ plus VAT) are entirely exempt from income tax and NICs provided three strict statutory conditions are met:
1. The payment is made directly by the employer to the independent solicitor.
2. The legal advice relates strictly to the termination of the employment and settlement terms.
3. The payment is made pursuant to a specific covenant within the formal settlement agreement.

If an employer reimburses legal costs directly to the employee instead of paying the solicitor firm, the exemption is extinguished and the reimbursement becomes fully taxable.

## 5. Case Study: How the Freeze Hits a Mid-Tier Redundancy in 2026

Consider a Senior Operations Manager earning **£65,000 per year** with **9 years of continuous service**, dismissed by reason of redundancy:

### Financial Package Breakdown:
- **Statutory Redundancy Pay:** 9 years at age 42 = 13.5 weeks x £751 weekly cap = **£10,138.50**
- **Agreed Ex-Gratia Severance:** 3 months gross salary = **£16,250.00**
- **Company Enhanced Redundancy Supplement:** **£12,000.00**
- **Total Compensation under Section 401:** **£38,388.50**
- **Notice Pay (PENP - 3 months unworked):** **£16,250.00** (Taxable as earnings)

### Tax Calculation Outcome:
1. **Notice Pay:** The £16,250 is taxed in full via PAYE (40% higher rate) and employee Class 1 NIC (8%).
2. **Termination Payment Total:** £38,388.50.
3. **Statutory Tax-Free Shelter:** First £30,000 is tax-free.
4. **Taxable Excess:** £8,388.50 is subject to 40% income tax = **£3,355.40** PAYE deduction.
5. **Employer Class 1A NIC on Excess:** 15.0% on £8,388.50 = **£1,258.28** paid by the employer.

If the threshold had been indexed to inflation since 1988 (standing at over £92,000), the entire £38,388.50 severance payment would have been sheltered, saving the employee **£3,355.40** in immediate take-home pay and saving the employer **£1,258.28** in payroll tax.
