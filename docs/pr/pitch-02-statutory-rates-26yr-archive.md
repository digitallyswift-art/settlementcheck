## 1. Executive Summary: The 26-Year Statutory Redundancy Trajectory

With the entry into force of the **Employment Rights (Increase of Limits) Order 2026 (SI 2026/310)** on 6 April 2026, the statutory weekly pay cap for calculating statutory redundancy pay and the unfair dismissal basic award in Great Britain has reached an all-time high of **£751 per week**.

SettlementCheck (`https://settlementcheck.co.uk/statutory-rates-history/`) has published an empirical 26-year historical study tracing statutory employment limits from 2000 to 2026. The findings uncover an unprecedented post-pandemic acceleration in statutory compensation ceilings, contrasted against a multi-decade freeze in tax relief thresholds:

1. **A +226.5% Increase in the Weekly Pay Cap:** The statutory weekly pay cap under Section 227 of the Employment Rights Act 1996 (ERA 1996) has expanded from **£230 per week in April 2000** to **£751 per week in April 2026**.
2. **The Maximum Statutory Redundancy Award:** The absolute statutory ceiling on redundancy pay (calculated on 20 years of qualifying service at a 1.5 age multiplier) has surged from **£6,900 in 2000** to **£22,530 in 2026**.
3. **The Unfair Dismissal Compensatory Award Cap:** The maximum compensatory cap under Section 124(1ZA) ERA 1996 has risen from **£50,000 in 2000** to **£123,543 in 2026** (+147.1%), while remaining bounded by the 52-week gross earnings cap introduced by the Enterprise and Regulatory Reform Act 2013.
4. **Post-2022 Inflationary Velocity:** Between 2000 and 2021 (a 21-year period), the weekly cap grew by £314 (an average annual increase of approximately £15 per year). In the five years between 2021 and 2026 alone, the cap grew by £207 (an average increase of over £41 per year), driven by statutory indexation to the Retail Prices Index (RPI) under Section 34 of the Employment Relations Act 1999.
5. **The Northern Ireland Divergence:** Under the Employment Rights (Increase of Limits) Order (Northern Ireland), the regional weekly pay cap stands at **£783 per week** from 6 April 2026, establishing a maximum statutory redundancy award of **£23,490** (a £960 premium over Great Britain).

## 4. Legal Practitioner Utility Guide: Managing Statutory Traps

For employment solicitors, in-house counsel, and HR leaders drafting or reviewing settlement agreements, the 2026 rates establish critical operational considerations:

### A. The Effective Date of Termination (EDT) Principle
Under Section 97 of the Employment Rights Act 1996, the statutory caps applicable to an employee's redundancy or basic award are strictly determined by their **Effective Date of Termination (EDT)**:
- If the EDT occurs on or before **5 April 2026**, the 2025/26 caps apply (£719 weekly cap, £21,570 maximum basic award, £118,223 compensatory cap).
- If the EDT occurs on or after **6 April 2026**, the new SI 2026/310 caps apply (£751 weekly cap, £22,530 maximum basic award, £123,543 compensatory cap).
- **Drafting Warning:** The date on which the settlement agreement is signed or the date when negotiations commenced is legally irrelevant. Only the statutory EDT establishes the baseline.

### B. PILON vs Garden Leave Timing Cliff Edge
Where an employee is dismissed with payment in lieu of notice (PILON), the statutory EDT can differ significantly:
- Under Section 97(2) ERA 1996, for statutory redundancy calculations, if an employer fails to give statutory minimum notice, the EDT is deemed to be extended by the statutory notice period.
- An employee terminated summarily on 31 March 2026 who is entitled to 12 weeks of statutory notice will have a deemed EDT falling in June 2026, entitling them to the higher **£751 cap**, not the legacy £719 cap.

### C. The High-Earner Decoupling Effect
While the statutory weekly cap has reached £751 (equivalent to an annualized salary of £39,052), median full-time managerial and professional salaries in London and corporate sectors exceed £65,000 to £95,000 per year.
- Because statutory redundancy covers only a fraction of high earners' actual earnings, negotiated settlement agreements routinely feature enhanced ex-gratia packages calculated on actual un-capped gross salary (such as one month of actual gross pay per year of service).
- The statutory archive demonstrates why high earners must rely on contractual and commercial negotiation rather than relying on statutory minimums.
