## 1. Executive Summary: The 2026 Employment Tribunal Backlog Crisis

Official Ministry of Justice (MoJ) quarterly tribunal statistics and HM Courts & Tribunals Service (HMCTS) caseload telemetry confirm that claimants and respondents in Great Britain now face procedural listing delays exceeding **48 weeks** to reach a final merits hearing across eight out of ten major urban hearing centres.

In severe administrative bottlenecks, delays approach eighteen months:
- **London Central (Victory House, Kingsway):** Median wait time of **540 days (77.1 weeks / ~17.8 months)** from ET1 claim lodgement to a multi-day final merits hearing, with **8,420 open single claims**.
- **London South (Montague Court, Croydon):** Median wait time of **510 days (72.9 weeks / ~16.8 months)**, with **6,150 open single claims**.
- **South East (Radius House, Watford):** Median wait time of **490 days (70.0 weeks / ~16.1 months)**, with **5,730 open single claims**.
- **Midlands West (Centre City Tower, Birmingham):** Median wait time of **420 days (60.0 weeks / ~13.8 months)**, with **4,940 open single claims**.
- **North West (Alexandra House, Manchester):** Median wait time of **380 days (54.3 weeks / ~12.5 months)**, with **5,210 open single claims**.

Across all ten surveyed regional centres, the aggregate outstanding single tribunal caseload exceeds **46,500 claims**. 

To provide objective public transparency for workplace parties, SettlementCheck (`https://settlementcheck.co.uk/tribunal-backlog-tracker/`) has launched the **2026 Employment Tribunal Backlog Tracker**. The tracker pairs live regional hearing delay datasets with empirical litigation defense cost models, illustrating why statutory settlement agreements under Section 203(3) of the Employment Rights Act 1996 have become the primary commercial default mechanism for resolving workplace disputes.

## 5. Commercial Deep-Dive: Why Pre-Claim Settlement is the Modern Workplace Default

The intersection of tribunal administrative friction and litigation economics has fundamentally altered the calculus of workplace exits:

```
Litigation Path (High Friction & Delay)
[Workplace Dispute] -> [ACAS Early Conciliation] -> [ET1 Lodgement] -> [150-Day Preliminary Wait] -> [390-540-Day Merits Wait] -> [Uncertain Award & Unrecoverable Costs]

Settlement Agreement Path (Certainty & Clean Break)
[Workplace Dispute] -> [Protected Conversation s.111A] -> [Draft Settlement Agreement] -> [Independent SRA Solicitor Review] -> [Immediate Clean Break & Tax-Sheltered Exit]
```

### The Cost-Benefit Calculus for Employers
1. **Unrecoverable Defense Expenditure:** Unlike the civil courts under the Civil Procedure Rules (CPR), where costs generally follow the event, the Employment Tribunal is predominantly a cost-free jurisdiction. Legal costs are awarded in fewer than 2% of resolved claims.
2. **The Median Award vs Defense Cost Paradox:** The median tribunal award for unfair dismissal in Great Britain stands at approximately **£7,564** (MoJ Annual Statistics). Meanwhile, defending that same claim through a multi-day hearing routinely costs between **£18,000 and £42,000** in professional fees. Settling pre-claim is therefore a rational commercial decision.
3. **Protection of Confidentiality:** Pre-termination negotiations conducted under Section 111A ERA 1996 offer confidentiality in unfair dismissal discussions. A signed Section 203 agreement provides finality through comprehensive statutory waivers and reasonable mutual confidentiality undertakings.

### The Decisive Benefits for Employees
1. **Elimination of Financial and Mental Limbo:** Waiting 12 to 18 months for an uncertain merits hearing subjects individuals to career stagnation, litigation anxiety, and delayed financial recovery.
2. **Tax Efficiency under ITEPA 2003:** Genuine compensation for loss of office benefits from the statutory £30,000 tax-free exemption under Section 403 of ITEPA 2003, whereas tribunal compensation awarded 18 months later may be eroded by tax, loss of earnings mitigation offsets, and personal expenditure.
3. **Agreed References and Mutual Annulment:** A tribunal cannot order an employer to provide a positive or agreed commercial reference. A settlement agreement standardly binds the employer to provide an agreed factual or professional reference, protecting the departing employee's future career prospects.
