# HMRC Employment Income Manual (EIM) & Statutory Tax Authorities

## 1. ITEPA 2003 Part 6 Chapter 3 — Payments on Termination

### Section 401 — Charge on Payments and Benefits
Applies to payments and benefits received in connection with the termination of a person's employment, unless charged to tax under another provision (e.g. general earnings under s.62).

### Section 403 — The £30,000 Threshold Exemption
- **Scope**: Applies only to payments that fall within s.401 and are not general earnings.
- **Exempt Portion**: The first £30,000 is exempt from Income Tax and employee National Insurance.
- **Excess over £30,000**: Subject to Income Tax at the employee's marginal rate (20%, 40%, 45% England/Wales; Scottish bands if resident).
- **Employee NIC on Excess**: **0% (Exempt)**.
- **Employer NIC on Excess**: **15.0% Class 1A NIC** (reported and paid in real time via RTI Full Payment Submission, effective 6 April 2025/2026).

### Section 413A & HMRC EIM13740 — Employer Legal Fee Contributions
Under HMRC EIM13740, an employer's contribution to the employee's legal fees is exempt from Income Tax and does NOT count towards the £30,000 exemption, provided **3 strict statutory conditions** are met:
1. **Direct Payment**: The payment must be made **directly to the employee's solicitor** (or other qualified adviser under s.203 ERA 1996). Direct reimbursement to the employee makes the payment taxable as general earnings.
2. **Exclusively Connected**: The payment must discharge a bill for legal costs incurred by the employee **exclusively in connection with the termination** of employment.
3. **Specific Clause in Agreement**: The payment must be provided for under a **specific term or covenant** within the written settlement agreement.
- *Exclusions*: Does NOT apply to accountancy, independent financial advice (IFA), or outplacement fees.

### Section 402D & HMRC EIM13874 — Post-Employment Notice Pay (PENP)
Since 6 April 2018, all payments in lieu of notice (PILON) are taxable as general earnings, regardless of whether there is a PILON clause in the employment contract.
Employers must calculate PENP using the statutory formula:
$$\text{PENP} = \left(\frac{\text{BP} \times \text{D}}{\text{P}}\right) - \text{T}$$
- **BP (Basic Pay)**: Basic pay in the last pay period ending before the trigger date (excluding bonuses, overtime, commission, benefits).
- **D (Days of Notice)**: Calendar days in the unworked notice period.
- **P (Pay Period Length)**: Calendar days in the last pay period (e.g., 30 or 31 days for monthly, or 30.42 average).
- **T (Taxable Amounts)**: Amounts already taxed as earnings in connection with termination (such as a contractual PILON).
- *Effect*: If PENP > 0, that exact amount is stripped out of the termination package and taxed in full as general earnings (PAYE + Class 1 Employee and Employer NIC). Only the remaining balance of the relevant termination award can benefit from the £30,000 exemption.

### Section 406 & HMRC EIM12810 — Disability and Injury Exemptions
- Payments made on account of an employee's death, disability, or injury are exempt from tax under Section 406.
- Under Section 406(2), "injury" does NOT include psychiatric injury or distress resulting from the termination itself.
- For discrimination cases, compensation for pre-termination injury to feelings may be tax-free, but injury to feelings directly resulting from dismissal is subject to Section 401.

## 2. National Insurance Contributions (HMRC NIM07000)

| Payment Component | Employee NIC Rate | Employer NIC Rate | Tax Treatment |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :--- |
| **Salary & Arrears** | Class 1 (8% / 2%) | Class 1 (15.0%) | 100% Taxable (s.62) |
| **Notice Pay / PILON / PENP** | Class 1 (8% / 2%) | Class 1 (15.0%) | 100% Taxable (s.402D) |
| **Accrued Holiday Pay** | Class 1 (8% / 2%) | Class 1 (15.0%) | 100% Taxable (s.62) |
| **Bonus / Commission** | Class 1 (8% / 2%) | Class 1 (15.0%) | 100% Taxable (s.62) |
| **Statutory Redundancy Pay** | 0% (Exempt) | 0% (Exempt) | Tax-exempt under £30k (s.403) |
| **Ex Gratia Termination Award (≤ £30k)** | 0% (Exempt) | 0% (Exempt) | Tax-exempt under £30k (s.403) |
| **Ex Gratia Termination Award (> £30k)** | 0% (Exempt) | **15.0% (Class 1A via RTI)** | Taxable at marginal rate |
| **Legal Fee Contribution (s.413A)** | 0% (Exempt) | 0% (Exempt) | 100% Tax-exempt (s.413A) |

## 3. Scottish Income Tax Bands (2026/27)

For employees whose main home is in Scotland:
- **Starter Rate (19%)**: Over £12,570 to £14,876
- **Basic Rate (20%)**: Over £14,876 to £26,561
- **Intermediate Rate (21%)**: Over £26,561 to £43,662
- **Higher Rate (42%)**: Over £43,662 to £75,000
- **Advanced Rate (45%)**: Over £75,000 to £125,140
- **Top Rate (48%)**: Over £125,140
- *Personal Allowance Tapering*: 60% marginal tax trap between £100,000 and £125,140 across all UK jurisdictions.
