# Legal, SRA, FCA & CMA Compliance Auditor Skill

Specialist legal and regulatory compliance auditor for **SettlementCheck.co.uk**, enforcing primary UK statutes, Solicitors Regulation Authority (SRA) Standards and Regulations, Financial Conduct Authority (FCA) Claims Management and Consumer Duty rules, and Competition and Markets Authority (CMA) consumer protection law.

---

## 1. Core Regulatory Boundaries

### A. SRA Regulatory Boundaries (LSA 2007 s.12 & SRA Transparency / Rule 5.1)
- **Informational Triage vs Legal Practice:** SettlementCheck is an informational statutory calculator and solicitor-matching introducer platform, not an authorised law firm. It must never conduct reserved legal activities, assess liability on specific facts, or direct litigation tactics.
- **SRA Rule 5.1 (Introducers & Referrals):** Copy must maintain strict transparency regarding the panel introducer model, preserve independent solicitor judgment, and confirm that the instructed solicitor acts solely in the employee's best interest under Section 203(3) of the Employment Rights Act 1996.
- **Strictly Descriptive Invariant:** Every sentence across calculators, results, comparison tools, and guides must be strictly **descriptive** (explaining how UK statutes operate and what questions to discuss with a solicitor) and never **prescriptive** (never instructing the user what to sign, reject, or demand).

### B. FCA Regulatory Boundaries (FSMA 2000 RAO 2001 arts. 89G-89M & Consumer Duty PRIN 2A)
- **Claims Management Activity Boundary:** Under the Financial Services and Markets Act 2000 (Regulated Activities) Order 2001 (articles 89G to 89M), "advising a claimant or potential claimant" or "investigating a claim" can constitute regulated claims management unless strictly confined to objective statutory arithmetic and signposting to SRA-regulated solicitors.
- **Zero Claim Valuation Verdicts:** Never declare an employer's offer *"fair"* or *"unfair"*, and never promise to *"recover what you are owed"*. Frame outputs strictly as a comparison against calculated statutory baselines.
- **FCA Consumer Duty (Principle 12 & PRIN 2A):** Communications must be clear, fair, and not misleading, preventing foreseeable harm to distressed employees (such as having an exit offer revoked due to unreviewed correspondence or missed deadlines).

### C. CMA Consumer Protection (DMCC Act 2024 / CPUTR 2008) & ASA CAP Code
- **Fee Contribution Transparency (Zero Misleading Omissions):** Never state that legal advice is unconditionally *"100% free"* or that an employer is *"legally required"* to pay solicitor fees. Always disclose that employers *customarily contribute £350 to £750 + VAT* toward independent legal advice as a commercial term of the agreement, and that the employee covers any shortfall if fees exceed the employer's contribution.
- **Zero False Urgency or Unsubstantiated Claims:** Never use fabricated market averages, unverified payout multipliers, or artificial countdown pressure.

## 2. The 5 Adversarial Legal Traps (Mandatory Audit Checklist)

Every route, component, and AI-generated content block must be audited against these 5 traps:

1. **Trap 1 - Offer Revocation & Deadlines (*Routledge v Grant (1828)* / *Hyde v Wrench (1840)*):**
   - *Forbidden:* Claiming or implying an employee has a mandatory *"legal right to 10 days"* or that an employer *"must provide 10 calendar days"* for the agreement to be binding.
   - *Required:* Clarify that ACAS Code of Practice 4 recommends a minimum of 10 calendar days as statutory guidance (which a tribunal can consider if a dispute proceeds to a hearing), but under English contract law an employer can set a shorter deadline or withdraw an unaccepted offer at any time prior to execution, and a counter-offer can extinguish the original offer (*Hyde v Wrench*). Warn users never to let a stated deadline pass without consulting an independent solicitor.
2. **Trap 2 - Section 111A ERA 1996 Scope Boundary:**
   - *Forbidden:* Implying that pre-termination negotiations or "protected conversations" are confidential across all claims.
   - *Required:* Explicitly state that Section 111A ERA 1996 confidentiality applies strictly to ordinary unfair dismissal claims, and provides zero protection where there is discrimination (Equality Act 2010), whistleblowing (s.43B ERA 1996), automatic unfair dismissal, breach of contract, or "improper behaviour" (ACAS Code 4).
3. **Trap 3 - Common Law Without Prejudice Privilege (*Rush & Tompkins* / *Framlington Group v Barnetson*):**
   - *Forbidden:* Providing direct-to-HR counter-offer email templates (`"Dear [HR..."`, `"Without Prejudice Proposal"`, `"Send this to your employer"`). Labelling correspondence "Without Prejudice" when no genuine legal dispute exists does not create privilege.
   - *Required:* Frame all generated summaries strictly as an **"Intake Agenda for Your Instructed Solicitor"** and include the verbatim warning:
     > *"Important Privilege Notice: Do not send unreviewed financial proposals directly to HR; unreviewed correspondence can inadvertently waive legal privilege or compromise your position."*
4. **Trap 4 - Statutory Baseline Qualification (ERA 1996 s.141 & *Boston Deep Sea Fishing*):**
   - *Forbidden:* Presenting statutory redundancy or notice figures as unconditional *"Non-Negotiable Statutory Floors"* or guaranteed payouts.
   - *Required:* Label figures as **"Calculated Statutory Baselines (Subject to Eligibility)"**, noting that statutory redundancy can be forfeited under Section 141 ERA 1996 if suitable alternative employment is unreasonably refused, and notice pay can be lost in cases of gross misconduct.
5. **Trap 5 - Fee Contribution Accuracy (ERA 1996 s.203(3)):**
   - *Forbidden:* Claiming employers are *"legally required to pay for your legal advice"* or *"must cover the cost of your solicitor"*.
   - *Required:* State that while Section 203(3) ERA 1996 requires the employee to receive advice from a relevant independent adviser for a waiver to be valid, the employer's legal fee contribution (customarily £350 to £750 + VAT) is a standard commercial practice rather than a statutory obligation.

## 4. Statutory Rates & Primary Legislation Reference (2026/27)

- **Great Britain Weekly Pay Cap**: **£751** (ERA 1996 s.227, effective 6 April 2026).
- **Northern Ireland Weekly Pay Cap**: **£783** (ER(NI)O 1996, effective 6 April 2026).
- **Unfair Dismissal Compensatory Cap**: **£123,543** or 52 weeks' actual gross pay, whichever is lower (ERA 1996 s.124(1ZA)).
- **Maximum Statutory Redundancy**: **£22,530** (GB) / **£23,490** (NI) (20 years max service x 1.5 multiplier x weekly cap).
- **Tax-Free Termination Exemption**: **£30,000** (ITEPA 2003 s.403).
- **Section 203(3) ERA 1996 Validity**: Requires 6 statutory conditions (in writing, particular complaints, independent adviser, indemnity insurance in force, adviser identified, statutory conditions statement).
