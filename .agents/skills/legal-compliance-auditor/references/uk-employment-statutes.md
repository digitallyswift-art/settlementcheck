# UK Employment Law Statutes & Regulatory Reference Guide

## 1. Primary Legislation & Case Law Authorities

### Employment Rights Act 1996 (ERA 1996)
- **Section 86**: Statutory minimum notice periods (1 week after 1 month of service; 1 week per complete year of continuous service from 2 to 12 years, capped at 12 weeks).
- **Section 111A**: Confidentiality of pre-termination negotiations. Applies strictly to ordinary unfair dismissal claims under s.94 ERA 1996. Does **not** apply to automatic unfair dismissal (e.g. whistleblowing under s.103A, maternity/pregnancy under s.99), discrimination under the Equality Act 2010, breach of contract, or where anything is said or done that constitutes "improper behaviour" (subsection 111A(4)).
- **Section 124(1ZA)**: Unfair dismissal compensatory award cap (lower of £123,543 or 52 weeks' gross pay from 6 April 2026).
- **Section 141**: Renewal of contract or re-engagement. An employee who unreasonably refuses an offer of suitable alternative employment forfeits the right to a statutory redundancy payment.
- **Section 162**: Statutory redundancy pay formula (0.5 weeks' pay per year under age 22; 1.0 weeks' pay per year aged 22-40; 1.5 weeks' pay per year aged 41+, capped at 20 years' service and £751/week in Great Britain).
- **Section 203(3) & (3A)**: Conditions regulating settlement agreements:
  1. The agreement must be in writing.
  2. The agreement must relate to the particular proceedings or particular complaints.
  3. The employee must have received advice from a relevant independent adviser as to the terms and effect of the proposed agreement and, in particular, its effect on their ability to pursue their rights before an employment tribunal.
  4. There must be in force, when the adviser gives the advice, a contract of insurance, or an indemnity provided for members of a profession or professional body, covering the risk of a claim by the employee in respect of loss arising in consequence of the advice.
  5. The agreement must identify the adviser.
  6. The agreement must state that the conditions regulating settlement agreements under the Act are satisfied.

### Income Tax (Earnings and Pensions) Act 2003 (ITEPA 2003)
- **Sections 402A-402E**: Post-Employment Notice Pay (PENP). Any unworked basic notice pay calculated under the statutory PENP formula `((BP x D) / P) - T` is subject to Income Tax and Class 1 National Insurance Contributions regardless of how the settlement agreement labels the payment.
- **Section 403**: Up to £30,000 of genuine non-contractual termination compensation (ex-gratia) is exempt from Income Tax and employee National Insurance, provided it is not PENP, contractual PILON, a restrictive covenant payment (s.225), or retirement benefits (s.393B).

### Key English Contract & Privilege Authorities
- ***Routledge v Grant* (1828) 4 Bing 653**: An offeror is entitled to withdraw or revoke an offer at any time prior to unconditional acceptance, even if the offeror previously stated the offer would remain open for a fixed period (unless supported by separate consideration). ACAS Code of Practice 4 (10 calendar days) is statutory guidance rather than an irrevocable contractual lock.
- ***Hyde v Wrench* (1840) 3 Beav 334**: A counter-offer operates in law as a rejection of the original offer and extinguishes it, meaning the offeree can no longer accept the original offer unless the offeror renews it.
- ***Rush & Tompkins Ltd v Greater London Council* [1989] AC 1280 & *Framlington Group Ltd v Barnetson* [2007] EWCA Civ 502**: Common law "Without Prejudice" privilege attaches only where there is an existing, genuine dispute between the parties and the communication represents a genuine attempt to settle that dispute. Merely writing "Without Prejudice" on an email when no live legal dispute exists does not confer privilege.
- ***Boston Deep Sea Fishing and Ice Co v Ansell* (1888) 39 Ch D 339**: Fundamental breach of contract (gross misconduct) entitles an employer to dismiss summarily without notice or payment in lieu of notice.

## 2. Regulatory Authorities & Rules

### Solicitors Regulation Authority (SRA)
- **Legal Services Act 2007 (s.12)**: Reserved legal activities (including the conduct of litigation). While non-contentious employment advice is not a reserved activity per se, holding out as a regulated legal practice or providing tailored legal advice without authorisation creates severe regulatory and civil negligence (*Hedley Byrne & Co Ltd v Heller & Partners Ltd* [1964] AC 465) liability.
- **SRA Standards and Regulations (Rule 5.1 - Referrals and Introductions)**: Introducers must not compromise the independence of the instructed solicitor. Users must be informed of how panel referrals operate and that their solicitor acts exclusively for them.

### Financial Conduct Authority (FCA)
- **FSMA 2000 (Regulated Activities) Order 2001 (Articles 89G to 89M)**: Seeking out, referrals, and identification of claims or potential claims in respect of employment matters is a regulated claims management activity unless it falls within statutory exclusions (such as pure information provision and signposting to regulated legal practitioners under Article 89N/89O).
- **FCA Consumer Duty (PRIN 2A)**: Communications must support consumer understanding and avoid foreseeable harm.

### Competition and Markets Authority (CMA) & ASA CAP Code
- **Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act 2024 (DMCC Act) & CPUTR 2008**: Prohibits misleading actions and misleading omissions regarding pricing and service terms. Specifically, stating that legal advice is "free" without disclosing that employers *customarily contribute £350 to £750 + VAT* (and that any excess is payable by the employee unless agreed otherwise) is a prohibited misleading omission.
